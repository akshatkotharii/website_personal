Aliasearch

A research skill for finding people or organisations worth speaking to—and showing why they might fit before drafting a message.

See the project on GitHub ↗

A long list can feel like progress. But who on it has a real reason to hear from you?

Aliasearch grew from a problem I kept noticing in outreach work: writing a message is often treated as the hard part, while research gets rushed. A name from a directory or search result may be a lead. It does not tell me whether that person is real, relevant, reachable, or a good fit for the actual offer.

I wanted a repeatable way to do that thinking carefully. Give Aliasearch a clear offer, audience, market, exclusions, desired count and evidence bar. It looks for candidates in appropriate public sources, keeps the evidence beside each decision and marks what is still uncertain. A draft message comes later, and only when asked.

Four steps before a first message

01 / FRAME

Say what you need.

Who the offer helps, where to look and what does not count.

02 / FIND

Search useful places.

Company sites, associations, directories and accessible public profiles.

03 / CHECK

Keep proof close.

Resolve duplicates, verify claims and preserve source context.

04 / DECIDE

Return a reviewable list.

Show fit and gaps so a person can choose the next step.

What I want to know before contact

IdentityIs this a real person or organisation in the right market?
NeedIs there evidence they experience the problem or serve the audience?
FitDoes their work connect to this specific offer, not just the industry label?
RouteIs there a credible public way to reach them, with its source recorded?

Why I made the evidence visible

Research can look more certain than it is. A polished spreadsheet may hide a weak source, an old fact or a guess about someone’s role. I wanted the tool to make those gaps easier to see.

Aliasearch keeps possible candidates separate from qualified prospects, marks missing evidence and does not fill a requested count with weak matches. If the brief asks for twenty strong leads and only seven meet the bar, I would rather return seven with reasons than twenty that only look complete.

A person stays in charge.

Aliasearch does not send messages, promise internet-wide coverage or claim reply rates. It makes a researched decision easier to review; the person decides whether and how to reach out.

What I am trying to learn

I like building systems that help people repeat careful work without hiding their judgement. Aliasearch is still early. I want to learn where its research breaks, which evidence is enough to make a decision, and how to keep the workflow useful without turning every search into a giant report.

If you do customer research or outreach, I would value hearing where this approach would help—or where it would slow you down.
