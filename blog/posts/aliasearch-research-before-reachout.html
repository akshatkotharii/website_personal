A project note · customer research

Aliasearch

A research skill that checks whether a potential customer fits a real offer, shows the evidence, and leaves the decision to a person.

See the project on GitHub ↗

A name in a list is a starting point, not a reason to reach out.

I built Aliasearch after seeing how easy it is for outreach research to become a search for volume. A directory gives you names. A quick web search gives you snippets. A large spreadsheet can look persuasive. None of those things alone tells you whether a company is real, whether it buys or resells the relevant product, or whether your offer fits its work.

The first practical task was to find plausible wholesale fabric buyers for a Mumbai textile manufacturer and exporter. The brief was specific, but the early research showed how quickly a candidate can look promising for the wrong reason. A company may work in textiles without buying this product. A directory may be out of date. A public profile may identify a person without showing that they handle purchasing.

That gap between finding a name and being able to defend why it belongs on a shortlist became the product problem. I wanted a repeatable process that makes the reasoning visible, including the places where evidence is thin.

Research first, message later

Aliasearch is a reusable skill for an AI assistant, not an autonomous sales agent. It starts with a brief: the actual offer, who might buy it, the relevant market, exclusions, the evidence standard and the number of strong matches requested. If those details are vague, the research should pause for clarification instead of quietly filling in assumptions.

01 / FRAME

Define the offer.

Clarify buyer, market, exclusions and what counts as evidence.

02 / DISCOVER

Find candidates.

Use relevant company sites, directories and public sources.

03 / VERIFY

Check each claim.

Deduplicate and record where identity, fit and contact details came from.

04 / REVIEW

Return a decision.

Show evidence and gaps so a person can choose what happens next.

Four questions before a prospect qualifies

A search result can help discover a company. Qualification asks for more. I use four checks to keep the decision grounded in the offer rather than a broad industry label.

Qualification checks

IdentityIs this a real organisation in the market described by the brief?
Buying roleIs there evidence it buys, imports, stocks or resells through a relevant business route?
Offer fitDoes its product range or use connect to this specific offer?
Contact routeIs there a credible public business contact path, with its source recorded?

These checks also make uncertainty useful. A candidate might pass identity but lack evidence of buying role. Another might fit the product but have no verified contact route. Those are different situations, and hiding both behind one green “qualified” label would make the result less trustworthy.

Evidence should travel with the recommendation

Reviews of the first research work found weak role evidence, uncertain product matches, stale or conflicting pages, and incomplete contact details. The issue was not that the search had found nothing. It was that the list did not always show which claims could be relied on.

Aliasearch keeps candidates separate from verified companies, preserves source context, and records the reason behind a decision. A result should make it possible to ask: what did we observe, where did that information come from, how recent is it, and what remains unknown?

That approach changes how I think about requested counts. If a brief asks for twenty strong prospects and only seven meet the bar, I would rather return seven with clear reasons than pad the list with weak matches. The number is part of the request, but it cannot make unsupported evidence stronger.

A person stays in charge.

Aliasearch helps research and prepare a reviewable shortlist. It does not send messages, guarantee complete coverage or claim that a prospect will respond. A person decides whether the evidence is sufficient and whether contact makes sense.

What I want the tool to become

I want Aliasearch to make careful research easier to repeat, not to automate away judgement. A useful next version should help a researcher move from an offer to a defensible list without burying the reasons inside a long report. The evidence should be compact enough to review and detailed enough to challenge.

It is still early. I need to learn where the qualification gates miss good prospects, where sources create false confidence, and how to keep the workflow focused when the brief itself is unclear. Better research may produce fewer names. That can be a better result if each one has a real reason to be there.

If you do customer research or sell to other businesses, I would value hearing where this process would help and where it would slow you down.
