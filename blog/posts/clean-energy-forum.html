A field note from the founding team

Building a place for clean-energy research to move.

I want more students to find serious energy questions, meet people working on them, and have a way to contribute.

Explore the current forum ↗

Clean energy is not a distant topic for India. It touches how homes stay cool, how businesses operate, how cities grow, and how reliable energy reaches people. Research and development in this area deserve more attention—and more people should be able to take part in the work.

That is why I joined the founding team of Plaksha Clean Energy Forum (CEF), powered by the Indorama Ventures Center for Clean Energy. My role is Research & Entrepreneurship Head. I care about building the connections that help a question move from “this matters” to “here is a project we can investigate together.”

The forum has started to take shape

Since the earliest version of this story, the public site has grown. It now points visitors toward research, events, dispatches, community, mentors, leadership and ways to work with the forum. Its join form asks what someone can contribute, why the problem matters to them, and how much time they can commit. I like that choice: collaboration needs both a reason to join and a realistic sense of the work involved.

Those pages are an invitation and a structure for growth. They are not evidence that every research programme or partnership is already in place. We are still building the relationships and work that should give each part meaning.

What I hope the forum makes easier

03 / CONNECTBring the right people inMake it easier for students to work with faculty, mentors and industry.
04 / SHARELet learning travelDocument what changed, what did not and what someone else can build on.

In my role, I want to help shape project ideas, find where student curiosity meets a useful research question, and make collaboration with mentors and industry more concrete. The challenge is to create an open door without making the work vague: a strong project needs a clear question, a method that can answer it, and honest limits around what the results mean.

My own work at IVCCE has made that connection feel practical. In my smart-energy nudging research, a technical system only becomes useful when it fits people’s routines, respects their choices and produces evidence we can trust. Research, product thinking and human context belong in the same conversation.

What I believeIndia needs more people doing careful clean-energy research—and more ways for students to join that work early.

Still early, still worth building

I do not want to describe the future as if we have already reached it. CEF is being built. The next work is to make the research questions sharper, create useful paths for student participation, and grow relationships with people who can help projects stand up to real-world constraints.

I hope the forum becomes a place where a student can arrive with a question, find collaborators, and learn how to take the next research step. If you work in clean energy—or want to help students get involved—I would love for you to explore the forum with us.
