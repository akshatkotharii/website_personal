A field note from the founding team

Building a place for clean-energy questions to move.

I joined because I want more students to find serious energy questions, meet people working on them and have a practical way to contribute.

Explore the current forum ↗

Clean energy feels like one of the defining areas to build in. For India, the questions are immediate: how to meet growing demand, make energy more reliable and affordable, build resilience, and develop the knowledge and capability to shape what comes next. I believe research and development deserve more attention, and more people should have a path into that work.

That is why I joined the founding team of Plaksha Clean Energy Forum (CEF), powered by the Indorama Ventures Center for Clean Energy (IVCCE). I serve as Research and Entrepreneurship Head. The role sits between a question, a project and what the work might become. I want to help make energy problems clear enough to investigate, and help notice where a useful idea could lead to a pilot, partnership or deeper research.

My own work at IVCCE has made this feel practical. A technical system can be carefully designed and still struggle when it meets real routines, deployment constraints or missing evidence. I want more students to get close to that process, where the question is not only “can we build it?” but also “who will use it, what would make it trustworthy, and what would count as learning?”

Why a forum, rather than another list of opportunities?

Students often have curiosity before they have a project. They may care about energy but not know which questions are researchable, who to speak with, or what a first contribution could look like. A list of events or links can help someone discover a field. It does not necessarily help them move from interest to work.

I hope CEF can become a place where that transition is easier. The forum can connect research questions with student effort, faculty and mentors, people in industry, and communities facing practical challenges. The relationships matter as much as the project page. A good project needs a clear scope, a way to test its assumptions and someone who can help it keep moving.

The path we are trying to build

01 / LISTENFind a real questionNotice challenges emerging from research, industry and communities.
02 / SHAPEMake it workableClarify what is known, what is uncertain and what can be tested.
03 / CONNECTBring people togetherHelp students find relevant collaborators and mentors.
04 / DOSupport the workKeep projects moving through check-ins and useful documentation.
05 / SHARELet learning travelRecord what changed and what a next team can build on.

This is a working direction, not a claim that every stage is already in place. The public site now gives people routes into research, events, dispatches, the community, mentors, leadership and a join form. That form asks what someone can contribute, why the problem matters to them and how much time they can commit. I like that it asks for a realistic commitment as well as enthusiasm.

The forum is still early. Some projects are beginning to roll out, and the work ahead is to learn from those first efforts. We need to understand whether project scopes are clear, whether students can make a meaningful contribution and whether collaborators have the support they need. A website can make an invitation visible; the quality of the work has to earn people's trust.

Where research and entrepreneurship meet

For me, research means helping identify worthwhile questions and making them specific enough to investigate. Entrepreneurship means noticing when an idea could have a useful life beyond one project: perhaps as an application, a pilot, a partnership or a venture. Neither should rush past the evidence. A promising possibility still needs to meet real constraints.

That balance is important in clean energy. A prototype can work under one set of conditions and still be difficult to deploy elsewhere. A student project can produce an interesting result without being ready for a real-world use. The forum should help people communicate those differences clearly, so each project can find the right next step rather than being pushed toward a premature success story.

What I believeIndia needs more careful clean-energy research, and more ways for students to join that work early.

What I hope we learn by building it

I do not want to write about CEF as if we have already built the community I imagine. We are at the start. The next work is to support the first projects, meet the people who join, keep the structure light enough to change, and document what we learn from doing the work.

I hope a student can arrive with a question, find people who help sharpen it, and leave with a clearer sense of what they want to investigate next. I hope researchers and industry collaborators can find students ready to work on meaningful problems. And I hope the forum becomes useful because of the projects and relationships it supports, not because of how ambitious its description sounds.

This is close to my heart because clean energy is not only a technology field. It is a long-term set of questions about how we live, build and make choices. I want to help create more room for students, mentors, researchers and industry to work on those questions together.
