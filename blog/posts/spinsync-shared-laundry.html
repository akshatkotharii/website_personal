SpinSync · a shared-laundry project

A free machine should not be a guessing game.

Six of us knew the routine: walk to the hostel laundry room, find every machine occupied, and plan another trip. We wanted to make that small daily system easier to understand.

A live view could help someone decide whether to go, wait, or join the next turn.

SpinSync grew during a two-semester Innovation Lab and Grand Challenge project at Plaksha University in Mohali. On the six-person team, I led solution design. We started with a booking-app idea, then looked more closely at what actually made the laundry room frustrating.

What students told us

In an April 2026 survey, 70 hostel residents described how they used the shared machines. The clearest signals were not subtle:

80%56 of 70 said clothes were left in machines after cycles.
73%51 of 70 found machines occupied when they wanted one.
87%61 of 70 physically checked whether a machine was free.

These are answers from our survey, not a campus-wide estimate or proof that a new system will fix the problem. They helped us see that laundry involves several connected moments: checking availability, taking a fair turn, noticing when a cycle ends, and making room for the next person.

Why booking was not enough

A reservation can say who planned to use a machine. It cannot prove that the machine started, that the cycle finished, or that clothes were collected. We began shaping SpinSync as a service around the physical machine and the people sharing it.

The concept combines a live machine view with bookings and queues, pickup reminders, reporting, and an admin path for faults or exceptions. A smart socket might help signal when a cycle starts or ends, but that signal needs testing. It cannot tell whether clothes were removed, and it cannot replace a manual fallback when the network or hardware fails.

Fairness shaped the design. A late pickup can happen for many reasons. The team considered a buffer, evidence-based reports and human review, rather than letting an uncertain sensor trigger an automatic penalty.

What comes next

We have finished the course project, then chosen to continue SpinSync independently. The next step is practical: refresh the survey, talk with hostel stakeholders, validate the socket signal, and propose a small pilot on a limited number of machines.

There is no public demo yet, and the concept has not been shown to reduce waiting, disputes or pickup delays in a real hostel. A pilot would need to measure those things before we could say it works. For me, the project is a good reminder that building a useful app often starts with asking what the real-world system needs to do when people, machines and plans do not line up neatly.
