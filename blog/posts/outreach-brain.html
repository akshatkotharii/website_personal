A product story · outreach

The next person should not have to start over.

I began with a spreadsheet, a small event team, and around 600 contacts. The list was not the hard part. Keeping the team in sync was.

Open the live workspace ↗

For one college event, two or three of us were coordinating a long contact list. Excel helped us collect names, sort colleges, and prepare messages. But the actual work moved through calls, personal phones, and conversations between teammates.

A row might say “contacted”. It did not always tell the next person who spoke to them, what they discussed, or when to follow up. If the owner changed, the conversation could lose its history. I first thought we needed a better list. Then I realised we needed a better handoff.

That became the shape of the tool. A team can import Excel contacts, catch duplicates and validation problems, assign ownership, record calls and notes, set reminders, and connect people to events or registrations. The goal is not to replace every spreadsheet. It is to keep shared work from depending on scattered memory.

The live app currently presents itself as Nudgen. I think of the original problem as Outreach Brain: how do we keep a team’s outreach thoughtful when the list gets bigger than one person can hold in their head?

I learned to treat permissions and exports as part of the product, not cleanup for later. People need different levels of access, and teams should be able to take their records with them. That is why the workflow also separates workspace roles, limits what a dashboard role can see, and keeps sending permissions distinct.

Two outreach teams tried versions of the tool, and conversations with people who work on user interfaces helped me question what belonged in it. Feedback kept bringing me back to a simple test: does this help the next teammate take a sensible next step?

What I am still careful about

The current WhatsApp connection is an unofficial bridge intended for controlled testing. Delivery indicators are provider-reported signals, not a guarantee of a person’s response. I do not want a product story to confuse a working feature with proof that it is ready for every team.

That is still the part I find interesting: a team does not need more activity for its own sake. It needs enough shared context to continue a conversation with care. I want to keep learning from teams who manage outreach in spreadsheets, and understand where this tool makes their work easier—and where it gets in the way.
